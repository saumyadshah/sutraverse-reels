Salty fafda, syrupy jalebi, one plate. Why does Gujarat eat them together?

Fafda is gram flour, ajwain and salt, served with fried green chillies, raw papaya sambharo and chutney.
The pair became famous through Dussehra: for many Gujarati families it is the Vijayadashami breakfast, with queues outside farsan shops from as early as 4 a.m.
Folklore says Lord Ram loved jalebi and Hanuman loved gram-flour dishes, and many believe the Navratri fast is best broken with besan.
