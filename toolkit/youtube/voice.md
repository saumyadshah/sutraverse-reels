## Who I am talking to

One person: an Indian in their 20s or early 30s, often living away from home, who grew up hearing bits of
the Ramayana and Mahabharata from grandparents but never read them. They scroll late at night, feel
overthinking, family friction, work stress and comparison, and would never sit through a pravachan.
They want the story first and a lesson they can use tomorrow, explained like a friend would, not a priest.

## How I actually talk

A warm, unhurried storyteller talking to one friend over chai. Hindi voice-over in everyday Hinglish
("socho", "yaar", "pata hai?", "matlab", "bas"), short sentences, real pauses, rhetorical questions,
direct "aap/tum". Slows down and softens for the shloka. Every on-screen word, subtitle, title and
description is English. Example opening (Dhruva, our second-best Reel): "Paanch saal ka bachcha… aur
use kaha gaya: tum is god ke laayak nahi ho."

## Words I never use

"unlock", "game-changer", "dive in", "let's get into it", "mind-blowing", "you won't believe",
"secret they don't want you to know", "science proves", "transcend", "paradigm", "hume chahiye ki…",
"viral" as a promise, shuddh textbook Hindi outside the shloka itself.

## Words I do use

"Socho…", "Pata hai?", "Aur sach kahun?", the character's name early, "The text says… / Tradition
holds… / For us today…", "one thing to try today", "Follow SutraVerse for daily life lessons from
ancient wisdom."

## What I will not claim

No invented verses, dates, numbers or miracles. No "science proves". Never present a folk version,
TV-serial scene or later text as the original scripture; label it. Every verse is shown with
Devanagari, IAST, an English translation and the exact reference, verified on two editions. No paid
promises of results. Deities are always shown and described with respect.

## My format

Animated 2.5D illustrated storybook Reels and YouTube Shorts, 60–85 s, 1080x1920, no presenter on
camera. Opens on a named scripture character in conflict (never on a modern scene), mirrors the
viewer's life in beat 2, tells the story, lands the verse slowly, ends with one action and one
question. No channel intro, no swearing. 9 Reels a week (one each weekday at 10:00, two on Saturday
and Sunday at 10:00 and 18:00), plus 4 carousels. Channels: Instagram @sutraverselife and YouTube
@sutraversehindu ("SutraVerse | Hindu Scriptures").
